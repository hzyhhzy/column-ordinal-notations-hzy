Two-chain quiet copying: a weaker local interface than a q ribbon
================================================================
2026-10-15. Original legacy Python, not a modified rule. This is a LOCAL
raw-copy theorem and a conditional actual forest interface. It is NOT a
closure theorem for the Hardy cone of R=I[0][3][0]. In particular the
mandatory final cut may expose a different controller not covered here.

1. The two chains
-----------------
For a controller core c of step L, put
  a=c[0], P=c[-L-1], e=c[-L], D=c[:-L], I=c[L:].
Call the controller two-chain if each of D and I separately is a
consecutive q chain, where q(r)=row_r[-2]. There is NO condition connecting
the last point of D with the first point of I, or on unpaired bridge points.

For ordinary short/medium/long lengths 2L-1, 2L, 2L+1, these are respectively
two disjoint chains with one bridge point, two adjacent chains, or two
chains overlapping in P. The raw map sends D to I position by position.
Thus this condition is strictly weaker than saying the whole core is a
q chain. The proved large-input failure in
original-audit-hardy-tail-ribbon-failure.txt does not by itself refute it.

Say Sat holds if every native-eligible row r satisfies q(e_r)<=p_r, with
its ordinary central pair p_r=row_r[-L_r-1], e_r=row_r[-L_r]. Assume:
  T1. Sat holds in the current state.
  T2. The first full raw copy is defined, and the active controller is
      two-chain.
  T3. No native-eligible row in the active source interval [P,N] has e_r=a.
All ordinary cores are strictly increasing, steps positive, and all
explicit q references are valid. These syntactic validity premises are
understood throughout.

LOCAL THEOREM. Arbitrarily many consecutive FULL RAW copies preserve
T1--T3 and the two-chain property of the working controller. Every scan
started after these copies and the final controller cut is the identity.

Proof of Sat. The raw map f is strictly increasing on its defined domain,
so a copied row has central pair f(p_r),f(e_r). If e_r<a, both its old
q(e_r) and p_r are below a and are fixed. If a<e_r<P, full-copy definedness
places e_r in D. It is not the first member. Its q parent is the preceding
member of D, and the q parent of f(e_r) is the corresponding preceding
member of I. Hence q(f(e_r))=f(q(e_r)). If e_r>=P, the copied row situated
at f(e_r) gives the same equality directly. In all cases Sat follows from
q(e_r)<=p_r. The omitted case e_r=a is exactly T3, not an implicit claim
that q commutes at the minimum.

Preservation of the controller chains. For consecutive x<y in D, y>a.
If y<P the D/I relation just used gives q(f(y))=f(x); if y=P, the copied
row at f(P) gives it. For consecutive points in I, the right point is
strictly above P for each ordinary shape, so its copied row gives the
same equality. Thus f(D) and f(I) are again consecutive q chains. The
next active interval consists entirely of newly copied rows. Injectivity
of f gives f(e_r)!=f(a), establishing T3 at its new minimum. Definedness
persists: old fixed low references stay below the new minimum, listed
middle references become listed controller images, and old high references
become high references. This is the raw domain induction from the forest
interface, not a scan invariant.

Finally a cut leaves all remaining cores and q arrows unchanged, so Sat
survives. A fresh scan starts with no record. Sat prevents every native
record; with no native record, no mark completion can fire. Thus that
one scan is the identity, whatever the retained mark masks are.

2. What this says about the explicit third Hardy controller
----------------------------------------------------------
For n>=3, write A=R[n], a_0=4, a_j=5+j(j-1)/2, N=|A|. Its row N-2 has
  c=(2,3,a_0,...,a_(n-3),a_(n-1),a_n,...,a_n+n-3), L=n.
The source chain is (2,3,a_0,...,a_(n-4)), omitting the a-part when n=3.
The image chain is (a_(n-1),a_n,...,a_n+n-3). Both are consecutive q
chains. The failure of the WHOLE ribbon occurs across the omitted bridge:
a_(n-3) is followed by a_(n-1), whose q parent is a_(n-2).

The two quiet raw rounds in the cited third-Hardy computation carry these
two chains to the final controller of R[n][n][n]. The needed chain edges
do not use the minimum exception, so the raw q-commutation calculation
applies to each edge. Therefore the exhibited third controller is still
two-chain for every n>=3. This does NOT prove T3 for its whole next source
interval, nor any preservation after another mandatory cut. It narrows
the obstruction; it does not remove it.

3. A separate raw ancestry lemma
-------------------------------
H1 means that the q forest is preorder-numbered (each rooted subtree is
a physical interval). H2 means every point of every core is a q ancestor
of its row. A defined full raw copy preserves H1 and H2.

Indeed P and all controller points are ancestors of N, so a is an
ancestor of P, and every row in [P,N] lies in P's subtree. For r>P,
q(r)>=P; its copied parent is the corresponding high copied parent.
The first copied row, at address N=f(P), attaches to f(q(P))<N. If P=a,
q(P)<a and is fixed. If P>a, q(P) is a listed middle point (it occurs in
the copied source row P), and f(q(P)) is an old controller image and
therefore an ancestor of old N. Such a point is on the right frontier
of the retained prefix 1..N-1. Attaching the isomorphic P-subtree there
preserves preorder numbering.

For H2, any old core point x>=P has its high image on the copied ancestor
path. For x<P, it is an ancestor of P. If x<a, then it is an ancestor of
a and of the controller image chain. If a<=x<P, definedness puts it in
the controller and H2 makes f(x) an ancestor of old N. Strict increase
gives f(x)<=f(q(P)) whenever x<=q(P), so these old image points occur in
the right order on one old ancestor chain. They are consequently ancestors
of the new copied row at N and of every copied descendant. This proves H2.

This lemma is about raw copies. It does not repeat the invalid claim that
H2 survives an arbitrary effective frozen-mark completion. The known
36-row actual counterexample fails precisely at that latter step.

4. Conditional open-mark safety during a quiet forest macro
-----------------------------------------------------------
Suppose, in ADDITION to the syntactic quiet hypotheses, that H1/H2 hold
and the source has a valid OPEN bounded certificate with accurate stars.
Use the raw forest macro of original-audit-root-forest-interface.txt.
The required parallel [e,N] packets must be supplied by an ACTUAL auxiliary
resource which transports BOTH controller N and its implicit cap N+1;
an ordinary one-endpoint E permission is not automatically such a resource.
No quantitative supply of this extra permission below kappa_4 is claimed.

For each raw-copy state that satisfies H1/H2 and Sat, every retained mixed
legacy star is open-safe. To see the strict inequality correctly, let
delta be its old paired source. The actual code tests f(delta)<=a. Since
f(x)>a for every defined x>=a, this forces delta<a, despite the code's
non-strict written <= test. If the bottom trace row is t<P and its source
endpoint is alpha=e(t), then delta=p(t). Because a is an ancestor of P,
H1 puts every node in [a,P] in a's subtree. The possibility
delta<a<alpha<=t<P would imply q(alpha)>=a, contradicting Sat at t.
Hence alpha<=a. This is exactly the open mixed-copy guard; equality is
allowed because equality of functions on V_alpha does not evaluate the
endpoint object alpha. All-high and wholly low traces have their usual
direct open transport proofs.

Parallel copies of a complete tail subtree preserve H1/H2, and any short
M output is a prefix of the corresponding full raw output, so the raw
ancestry proof applies. The two-chain/Sat/T3 calculation must hold for
each active forest template: it holds when templates are the transported
parallel copies in the raw-forest induction and all of its copied source
rows satisfy T3. Stating this condition explicitly avoids inferring it
from the selected extraction alone. Under that condition every deferred
scan is quiet, so no unproved effective-mark ancestry closure is used.

Finally extract the selected complete syntax. For an ALREADY valid open
certificate, original-audit-open-extraction-repair.txt repairs implicit
right endpoints while preserving each bounded factor function exactly.
It retains the final owner and cap. Combining these local facts gives a
conditional actual realization of that one quiet original A[k] macro.

The unresolved global obligations are still substantial: a bounded actual
two-endpoint forest supply, T3 for successive exposed controllers, and
closure through nonquiet original scans. No statement here says that the
entire Hardy-tail cone of R satisfies them.

5. Exact boundary for preservation of ALL row chains
---------------------------------------------------
Suppose every source row is two-chain, and the active controller is
two-chain. For a particular copied row and either of its two chains,
every consecutive edge x<y is preserved by f EXCEPT POSSIBLY when y=a.
For y<a both ends are fixed; for a<y<P use the active D/I chains; for
y>=P use the copied row at f(y). These are all possible cases. Thus the
following additional full-band condition suffices to preserve two-chain
for every copied row, not just the next working controller:

  T4. The controller minimum a is never a NONFIRST member of either
      D_r or I_r of a copied row r in [P,N].

More precisely, if such an exceptional edge exists then x=q(a)<a and
its image survives exactly when q(f(a))=q(a). Under H2 this equality is
impossible: a and f(a) are controller core points, hence a is an ancestor
of f(a)>a, and q(f(a))>=a>q(a). Therefore, under H2, T4 is NECESSARY as
well as sufficient for preservation of all row two-chains by a raw copy.

Likewise, under H2, every copied eligible row with e_r=a becomes genuinely
unsaturated: its p_r<a is fixed while q(f(a))>=a. Therefore T3 is exactly
the Sat boundary in this setting, not merely a convenient sufficient
condition. A positive scan repairs this boundary by native completion;
one must then prove the resulting new marks/packets compatible with the
next controller. Avoiding it is not the same as solving it.

T3 and T4 are distinct. For a medium row, its first image e_r is the FIRST
member of I_r, so e_r=a need not violate T4. For a short row, e_r is the
unpaired bridge and belongs to neither D_r nor I_r. This explains the
actual n=2 Hardy diagnostic: after five positive steps the next copy has
the unique T3 witness row 3, core (0,1,2,3):2, e_r=a=2, while both chains
of every row remain intact. Its next native completion is unavoidable.

Companion original-audit-hardy-two-chain-probe.py checks just three fixed
short Hardy prefixes: n=2 first fails T3 at step 5; n=3 and n=4 preserve
the tested weak interface through 18 and 13 steps, respectively, before
the explicit 1200-row resource bound stops them. Runtime 0.125 seconds,
maximum just-constructed buffer 1455 rows/12293 fields. This is diagnostic
only. In particular it does not show that initial n>=3 excludes a later
minimum-boundary native event, and no long descent is proposed here.
