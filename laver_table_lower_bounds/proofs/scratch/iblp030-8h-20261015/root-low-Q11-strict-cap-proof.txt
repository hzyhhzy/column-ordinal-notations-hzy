A strict-sup cap for the low open-fan type-two staircase
======================================================
2026-10-15. New local theorem; NOT the full original-R Hardy target.
The first independent algebra audit by the Hardy sibling passed.

Notation is exactly that of the existing C[4] and low open-fan papers:
  K=j11, b=K(j), c=K(b), d=c(K), A=c(d),
  mu=K(kappa2), a=K(mu), theta8=j9(kappa3),
  s=j(<kappa1), v=K(<kappa2)=j10(s).
Application is F(G); superscript 2 or repeated point application below
means ORDINARY COMPOSITION. In particular c^2(x)=c(c(x)).
The cut v is not asserted to be a generated critical point.

THEOREM.
                A(v) < K^2(v) < theta8 < kappa4.          (1)

Proof.
The published approximation K equivalent to j o j below mu gives
j^2(t)=K(t) for every t<kappa2: K(t)<K(kappa2)=mu.
Consequently their strict suprema at kappa2 agree:
                (j o j)(<kappa2)=K(<kappa2)=v.             (2)
This uses values strictly below the cutoff, not an equality at mu.

There is an EXACT environment identity
                c=(K o K)(j),
because ordinary composition acts on the embedding algebra by successive
application. Hence, by distribution over ordinary composition,
                c o c=(K o K)(j o j).
Apply the strict-sup transport identity to (2). Since
                (K o K)(kappa2)=K(mu)=a,
we get the exact cut value
                (c o c)(<a)=(K o K)(v)=K^2(v).            (3)

Regularity/discontinuity gives v<mu. Thus K(v)<a. As a is a limit,
strict increase gives c^2(K(v))<c^2(<a). Also c fixes v because
crit(c)=mu. Application compatibility at this fixed input gives
                d(v)=c(K)(v)=c(K(v)),
                A(v)=c(d)(v)=c(d(v))=c^2(K(v)).           (4)
Equations (3)--(4) yield the FIRST strict inequality in (1).

For the second, use K=j10(j) and j10=j9(j), always at genuine
j10- or j9-image arguments:
  K^2(v)=K^2(j10(s))=j10(j^2(s))=j10(K(s)).
Here j^2(s)=K(s) is justified since s<kappa2 and K(s)<mu.
The standard strict-sup identities also give
  K(s)<K(<kappa2)=j10(s)<j10(<kappa2)=j9(s).
Therefore
  K^2(v)=j10(K(s))<j10(j9(s))=j9(j(s))<j9(kappa3)=theta8.
The last comparison uses s<kappa2, hence j(s)<kappa3. The published
point inequalities give theta8<kappa4. This proves (1).

Low-staircase consequence.
Let Q11=A(K). Its helper is a, its first outer entry is c_exit=theta7,
and every low open-fan initialized cap is strictly below Q11(<a).
Strict-sup transport gives
                Q11(<a)=A(K(<kappa2))=A(v).
Consequently ALL such finite initial caps lie below theta8<kappa4.
The helper a fixes the lower scalar domain kappa3 and moves the previous
type-one admission a; the first outer entry c_exit accommodates the
preceding output. Thus the previously proved low-staircase type-two
interface now has its missing cap guard.

Scope.
The initial argument established a cheaper actual type-two staircase
than the older E(V0) construction. The stronger continuation below DOES
also prove A(mu)<kappa4. Neither result produces all finite type levels,
the full first-root diagonal C, or original R=I030. No equality of A(<v)
and A(v), or use of an open cutoff at its endpoint, occurs.

Primary inputs are the approximation and strict-sup rules visually
checked in Dougherty, Critical Points in an Algebra of Elementary
Embeddings, printed pp.22--23: https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9205202 .
The rest is the displayed application/composition algebra.

Stronger continuation (independently audited by both siblings).
---------------------------------------------------------------
In fact
                 A(<a)<K^2(v)<theta8.                    (5)
This proves A(x)<theta8 for EVERY x<a, including the actual generated
critical points A(mu) and A(theta5), formerly unknown.

Indeed A=(c o c)(K) EXACTLY, since d=c(K). Also c^2(mu)=a.
Strict-sup transport therefore gives
                 A(<a)=c^2(K(<mu)).
The point mu is regular and K(mu)=a>mu, so the standard discontinuity
lemma gives K(<mu)<a. Consequently
                 A(<a)<c^2(<a)=K^2(v)<theta8.
This does not contradict A(a)>kappa4: that is a value at a large jump,
whereas (5) only concerns strictly smaller inputs.

Consequences for initialized low-fan caps:
  Q11 o V0: helper c_exit, entry A(mu), closed cap A(theta5)<theta8.
  Q11 o Q11: helper A(mu), entry A(theta5), strict initialized cap
      < A((K o K)(<kappa2)) < theta8.
The latter uses (K o K)(<kappa2)<(K o K)(kappa2)=a; regularity applies
again. The coarse cap A(a)>kappa4 is NOT used.

A useful actual critical upper endpoint for the latter cut is
                 rho0=j10(gamma3)<a,
because, using K=j10(j) and kappa2=j10(kappa1),
  (K o K)(<kappa2)=j10((j o j)(<kappa1))<j10(gamma3)=rho0.
Here (j o j)(<kappa1)<gamma3 is exactly the published strict-sup
inequality, not a point-value claim j(j(s))<gamma3.
Also j10(kappa3)=a: apply K=j10(j) at j10(kappa2)=mu to obtain
K(mu)=j10(kappa3). Hence A(rho0)<theta8 is a generated critical point
available as a closed endpoint rather than the coarser theta8.
