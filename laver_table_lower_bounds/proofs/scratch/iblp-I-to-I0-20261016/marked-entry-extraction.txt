Marked entry into paper-M: successor-closed forest extraction
============================================================
2026-10-16. This note concerns INITIALIZATION of the exact original
S=R[k][n], k,n>=2, in the paper natural-cutoff domain. It does not claim
that every later original IBLP step has a paper-M simulation. In
particular it is not a completed Hardy/FGH comparison for original R.

All actual embeddings below belong to the SAME Iter(j). All named
cardinals and the final cap remain below the supplied common bound C.
Application is G(F); ordinary composition is written with a circle.

1. A stronger repair identity than the old open-map observation
-------------------------------------------------------------
For embeddings G,F and c=crit(G), one has

                  G(F) equiv_c F,                            (1.1)

where equiv is Laver MEMBERSHIP cutoff:
  A equiv_c B iff for all x,z in V_c,
                    x in A(z) iff x in B(z).
Indeed G(x)=x and G(z)=z. Application covariance gives
G(F)(z)=G(F)(G(z))=G(F(z)). By elementarity,
  x in G(F(z)) iff G(x) in G(F(z)) iff x in F(z).
There is no assumption F(z) in V_c. This last point is why (1.1)
proves the required membership statement rather than merely an ordinary
open function restriction.

Suppose an old row r has implicit cap c=theta_(r+1)=F(alpha),
where alpha is its central source endpoint. A selected extraction changes
its next named point to c'>c but retains all explicit row points. If G
has crit(G)=c and G(c)=c', then F'=G(F) realizes exactly the same explicit
edges and the new implicit edge alpha -> c'. Every other row target
is <c, so those edges are unchanged. Also F' equiv_c F by (1.1).
If c'=c, keep F unchanged.

2. Successor-closed-factor extraction theorem
-------------------------------------------
Assume a finite actual marked pattern P has strong natural certificates.
Choose a set U of rows, including the required initial two rows, with:

  E1. Every explicit point/reference of a selected row is selected (or
      is the fixed initial point 0); all factors of retained exact
      p-traces are selected; all their syntactic trace arrows commute
      with increasing reindexing.
  E2. For EACH factor y occurring in any retained mark word, the physical
      point y+1 is also selected. Thus the successor of y is unchanged,
      not merely the next SELECTED point after y.
  E3. The final retained row and its old implicit cap are retained.

The implicit cap may be treated as a separately retained endpoint, not
as an additional row. Every cap repair in section 1 is available in
the same Iter(j), as is proved constructively in section 3.

CONCLUSION. After increasing reindexing and repairing the implicit caps
of the selected rows independently, the exact extracted syntax again
has strong natural membership certificates. Its last owner and final
cap are unchanged. In particular the extraction introduces no final
potential increase.

PROOF. A factor row y is not repaired: E2 retains exactly its old next
point theta_(y+1). Consequently every factor in every retained word W
is globally the old embedding, and W itself is unchanged. Its natural
cutoff is
  Delta(W)=j_(v0) circle ... circle j_(v_(t-1))(theta_(v_t+1)).
The final factor and its next point are unchanged, so Delta is unchanged.
The inherited trace remains the exact trace by E1. The natural-cutoff
bound says
                  Delta(W)<=theta_(y0+1)<=theta_r<c,
where y0<r is the mark point in carrier row r and c=theta_(r+1) is that
carrier's OLD implicit cap. If the carrier is repaired, (1.1) therefore
gives F' equiv_Delta F equiv_Delta W. If the carrier is itself a factor
elsewhere, E2 prevents its repair, so there is no simultaneous dependency
problem. The remaining actual row and critical-point equations follow
from section 1. E3 leaves the last owner untouched. This proves the
theorem. No global equality between a repaired carrier and a factor is
needed; the strong membership identity is exactly what is preserved.

This theorem is not valid for arbitrary reference-closed extraction.
If a factor's physical successor is skipped, both the factor's owner
and its natural cutoff can change; ordinary open agreement is then not
enough. That was the gap in applying the old open-extraction lemma
directly to the paper marked domain.

3. Endpoint transport with no unknown search or M waiting
-------------------------------------------------------
Qi's Lemma 2.11 gives an explicit finite construction using the ORIGINAL
actual forest, before any owner is repaired. Here is the recursion.

For every adjacent pair theta_i<theta_(i+1), build an embedding T_i with
  crit(T_i)=theta_i, T_i(theta_i)=theta_(i+1).
The first standard row supplies T_0. Having constructed the earlier T's,
inspect row i. Its last two target points are theta_i,theta_(i+1), so
its owner F_i sends two known source points theta_u<theta_v, with
u<v<=i, to these two target points. From previously available adjacent
transporters construct G_(u,v):theta_u -> theta_v by the recurrence
  G_(u,u+1)=T_u,
  G_(u,t+1)=T_t(G_(u,t))       (u+1<=t<v).
Application covariance and the critical-point rule prove each step.
Then set
                         T_i=F_i(G_(u,v)).
This is the required adjacent transporter. All dependencies have indices
strictly below i. Finally compose the T's by the same finite recurrence
to obtain G_(p,q) for every gap theta_p<theta_q to be repaired.

Thus the repair has an explicitly indexed finite straight-line recipe,
using only the supplied finite pattern's own rows and index intervals.
It does not enumerate critical points until an unknown event, and it
does not iterate modify/M until a canonical form appears. All actors
remain in Iter(j). These algebraic initialization constructions are not
being counted as paper-M transitions and do not manufacture extra
counted descent steps. They merely prove membership in the admissible
actual-pattern domain below the same final cap.

4. Exact forest induction for factor-successor closure
------------------------------------------------------
Write SC for: every mark y of every selected carrier r has BOTH y and
y+1 selected. For the present entry all marks are direct. The following
proof does not need an extra claim that the final selected set is convex
in every transported family.

At the first R[k] forest the raw state has no marks. The root-specific
native-only scan theorem selects every row of each family born at a
selected raw row. Its marks point to earlier members of that same
family; y+1<=r therefore lies in the selected family. This proves SC.

At the second forest the initial selected prefix is contiguous. Each
parallel tail packet is a union of two whole native families; their
direct marks point within those families. Thus every selected head in
that prefix has its successor selected, and every pending live packet
has all its own marks internal to the packet.

There are only two ways to add selected rows in the exact forest macro:

  A. Select all of the current active packet except its controller.
     The selection is a contiguous interval. Every direct mark of a
     retained row has its head y in that packet and y+1<=r strictly
     below the omitted controller. Hence both y and y+1 are selected.

  B. Select images of already selected source rows under a short copy.
     If copied carrier r retains mark y, the all-factor-high lemma in
     section 5 gives P<=y<r<e. Inductive SC supplies selected source
     row y+1, which also belongs to the source interval [P,e).
     The high map is translation, so f(y+1)=f(y)+1. Thus the image of
     the selected source successor is exactly the physical successor
     required for SC at the new factor. Old selected rows and their
     physical successors are unchanged.

Whole live packets transported by this same copy retain the internal
direct-mark property. The final remaining packet minus its controller
is case A. The final controller cut cannot remove a successor y+1 of
any surviving carrier, since y+1<=r<the cut row. Induction on precisely
n-1 short copies proves SC for the second forest. This is the needed E2.

It is not necessary to repair an entire equal-owner family in lockstep.
The all-factor-high premise is essential: this argument is not a theorem
for a general retained mixed legacy mark.

5. Application status for the exact two-level original entry
----------------------------------------------------------
For the first R[k] forest, all old marks are empty; the deferred scan is
native-only. The old all-index root-forest proof selects every created
native family in full. Within a family the marks are direct and all
owners are literally the same. They therefore have strong natural
certificates before extraction, without using any questionable legacy
mixed-copy guard. Section 4 gives E2 and section 2 yields an actual
strong-natural realization of EXACT original R[k] below the same cap.

For the second R[k][n] forest, the exact quiet-copy syntax and scan
identity for all k,n>=2 were established in
  original-audit-root-forest-interface.txt, section 5.
For the chosen application k,n>=3 is sufficient. The native triangular
formula has controller minimum a=2 and every direct mark's paired source
delta>=3>a. In any copied marked row, suppose more generally delta>=a.
If head y>=P, the factor is high and copied in full. If y<P then either:
  * y<a, impossible because delta<y and delta>=a; or
  * a<=y<P, where the only remaining legacy branch requires f(delta)<=a.
    But f(delta)>=f(a)>a, so it rejects the mark.
Thus every retained copied mark is direct and all-high. At the next
active source block the minimum is f(a), and every retained paired
source is f(delta)>=f(a), so the induction repeats. Complete parallel
packets preserve the starting inequality and their internal marks.
This directly checks the actual _copy_mark_filter branches, not just
an inferred resemblance to the paper guard.

On a short-copy source [P,e), a retained head y and carrier r satisfy
P<=y<r<e; hence y+1 is also copied and f(y+1)=f(y)+1. Carrier, factor,
and final-factor successor all transport under the same actual actor.
Application congruence therefore transports the STRONG natural cutoff
certificate exactly. There is no appeal to open bounded maps here.

Section 4 gives E2 for the final selection. The quiet scan is the
identity, so it introduces no additional issue. Section 2 consequently
gives exact original S=R[k][n] in the strong paper domain. This agrees
with the independent proof in marked-entry-initializer.txt, sections
2--4; its branch analysis has been checked against original iblp.py.

The entry line/parallel-packet resource and cap<kappa_4 are separate
inputs from local-budget2-initializer.txt. This note does not create
those resources. Subject to those previously audited actual resources
and the ordinary forest/native realization lemmas, no strong-natural
membership gap remains at extraction for the exact two-level entry.

6. What the entry would and would not give
----------------------------------------
If exact S has the strong-natural certificate, the later descent may
use PAPER short M and cuts. It need not agree with later legacy IBLP
expansions. The previously proved bounded last-owner potential can then
bound that paper descent by <2^q(5), with its own endpoint convention.
This is an admissible paper-descent bound for exact original S, not a
claim that it equals or dominates an original-IBLP Hardy computation.
An independent numerical simulation/comparison is still required for
the latter. In particular no passage from 'the input is R[k][n]' to
'its future paper trajectory computes H_R(k)' is licensed here.
