# BMS tower bounds at the smaller endpoint $\eta$ · [中文](bms.zh-CN.md)

Assume ZFC + I3 and retain the [common entrance](endpoints.md). Let

$$
\eta=((J_{11}(J_3))(J_7))(\gamma_3)=\gamma_s,\qquad
C=I[0][2][1]^8,
$$

where $I$ and its fundamental sequences are the repository's fixed [original IBLP](../code/iblp_original.py), and $[1]^8$ means eight successive operations. We prove

$$
\boxed{s>F_{C[6]}(M-1)+1,\qquad M=\mathcal Q(8)\ge2048}
\tag{1}
$$

In particular $s>F_{C[6]}(2047)$ and $s>F_C(6)$. Section 4 defines $\mathcal Q$ independently using fast BMS operators, without $s$ as a parameter. The common entrance proves

$$
\eta<J_{13}(\gamma_{16})<J_{11}(\kappa_3).
\tag{2}
$$

The argument reuses the existing relative BMS compiler and whole-type numerical comparisons. The new entrance, strict supremum bound, and type seams are checked below. A bound at a higher endpoint alone would not imply (1).

## 1. Supremum control in the new low background

The common entrance satisfies

$$
\operatorname{crit}q=\kappa_2,\quad q(\kappa_2)=\mu,\quad
q(\kappa_3)=\chi,\quad q(\mu)=\Sigma.
$$

Keep its $g,g_i,b_0,e,J,k,x,\delta,\xi$, and let $T_0=q(J_{10})$. Transporting $J_{10}\equiv_\mu J_8(J_2)$ along $q$ gives

$$
T_0\equiv_\Sigma b_0(g)(g_2).
$$

Since $g\equiv_{\kappa_2}j$, we get $b_0(g)\equiv_\xi e$ and hence $T_0\equiv_\xi J$. Using $J_{11}\equiv_\mu j\circ j$,

$$
k=J(g)\equiv_\xi T_0(g)=q(J_{11})\equiv_\Sigma g\circ g.
$$

Thus $k\equiv_\xi g\circ g$. For $z<\mu$, the strict guard is $k(z)<k(\mu)=\xi$, so

$$
k(z)=g(g(z)),\qquad v:=k(<\mu)=(g\circ g)(<\mu).
\tag{3}
$$

Let $s_1=j(<\kappa_1)$ and $s_2=j(<\kappa_2)$. The required strict source chain is

$$
\begin{aligned}
J_7(s_1)&=J_8(<\kappa_3)>J_8(s_2)
=J_9(<\kappa_2)>J_9(s_1)\\
&=J_{10}(<\kappa_2)>J_{10}(s_1)
=J_{11}(<\kappa_2)>J_{11}(s_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

These are the small-term and supremum identities of [Dougherty I, printed pages 22–24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9205202#page=22). The embedding $q$ fixes $s_1$, and $e$ fixes $s_1,\kappa_1,\mu$. Consequently

$$
g(<\kappa_1)=x(<\kappa_1)=s_1,\quad
x(\kappa_1)=J(\kappa_1)=\mu,\quad
v=J(s_1),\quad J(<\mu)=x(s_1).
$$

Since $s_1<\mu$ and $\mu$ is a limit ordinal, $k(s_1)<v=J(s_1)<x(s_1)$. Hereafter $k^2$ denotes ordinary composition $k\circ k$. Using $k\circ J=J\circ g$, $J=x(x)$, and $k(s_1)=g^2(s_1)$,

$$
k^2(v)=J(g^2(s_1))=J(k(s_1))
<J(x(s_1))=x(x(s_1))=e(k(s_1)).
$$

Equations (3), (4) also give

$$
k(s_1)=q(J_{11}(s_1))<q(J_7(s_1))=b_0(s_1).
$$

As $b_0(s_1)$ is a limit ordinal and $e=b_0(j)$,

$$
e(k(s_1))<e(<b_0(s_1))
=b_0(j(<s_1))<b_0(\gamma_3)=\eta.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{k^2(k(<\mu))<\eta.}
\tag{5}
$$

## 2. A uniform strict bound for all finite outputs

Define actual application terms

$$
b=k(g),\quad c=k(b),\quad d=c(k),\quad e_1=d(g),\quad f=e_1(k),
$$
$$
A=c(d),\quad V=c(e_1),\quad Q=A(k),\qquad
t=k(\chi),\quad a=k(\xi),\quad c_{\rm out}=k(t).
$$

LD and the composition laws give

$$
c=(k\circ k)(g),\quad c^2=(k\circ k)(g\circ g),\quad
c^2(\xi)=a,\quad A=c^2(k).
$$

Hence

$$
c^2(<a)=k^2((g\circ g)(<\mu))=k^2(v).
$$

The generated critical point $a$ is regular. Since $\xi<a=k(\xi)$, we have $k(<\xi)<a$. Supremum covariance at the actual image $a=c^2(\xi)$ yields

$$
\boxed{A(<a)=c^2(k(<\xi))<c^2(<a)=k^2(v)<\eta.}
\tag{6}
$$

This uniformly controls every $z<a$. It assumes neither $A(a)<\Sigma$ nor global equality in place of $q\equiv_\Sigma q_{\rm old}$.

## 3. Two actual bands and six type environments

### 3.1 Scalar level

In the [general front-insertion lemma](../proofs/output/iblp-laver-bms-20261013/lined-front-insertion.zh-CN.md) and the [low open-fan construction, Sections 1–3](../proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/lift-low-open-fan-and-typed-gap.txt), replace
$(j,\kappa_2,\kappa_3,J_{10},J_{11},\gamma_3)$ by
$(g,\mu,\chi,J,k,\delta)$.

This uses the lemma's general interface; it does not identify $J$ with the tenth left power generated by $g$. Section 5 of the common entrance verifies
$\operatorname{crit}J=\kappa_1$, $J(\kappa_1)=\mu$, $J(\mu)=\xi$,
$k=J(g)$, $k(\kappa_1)=\chi$, and $k\equiv_\delta g_3$, supplying low-owner reconstruction and every strict truncation reading.

The actual embedding $b(b)(k)$ sends $(\kappa_0,\kappa_1,\mu)$ to $(\mu,\xi,t)$. Given any actual $m$-line, successive front insertions produce an open fan of height $m+1$, preserving every positive port's implicit last edge and the spare port. The endpoint is at most $t$, and strictly below $t$ if $m\ge2$.

Take the $f$-image of the whole fan and lower port 1 in both actual bands. The resulting seed is exactly

$$
B_m=(0^m)(1^m).
$$

The scalar argument exits at most at $\chi$, and the object output at most at $a$. Every spare connector is an ordinary composition with actual global images. The shared membership cutoff reads all middle images after lowering the ports, preserving the implicit edges. The transport formulas are in Sections 2–3 of the open-fan manuscript and [Sections 1–5 of the spare-band proof](../proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/lift-participating-bms-spare.txt).

This is the complete entrance of the [natural relative BMS compiler, Sections 2–4](../proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/lift-natural-relative-bms-oracle.txt), supplying the all-input, all-external-environment contract for
$\mathsf S(u)(m)=K_{B_m}^{(0,u)}(m)$. Its induction uses BMS well-ordering and actual copying trees, not fictitious owners of virtual oracle endpoints.

Also

## 4. Natural input and the actual spectrum-index bound

The fast BMS operator $K_M^{(r,u)}$ has zero-index value $u$, iterates $2^m$ times at a successor with final integer input $m$, and uses original BM4 $M[2^m]$ at a limit. Higher types iterate whole lower-type objects. Exact finite types, diagonals and tail order are given in the [type comparison](../proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/hardy-root-typed-bms-bound.txt), [same-input fast comparison](../proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/hardy-root-fast-type1-C3.txt), and [original tower paper, Sections 1–4](../01-blp-tower.zh-CN.md).

Define

$$
f_0(m)=m2^m,\quad
\mathsf S(u)(m)=K_{B_m}^{(0,u)}(m),\quad
\mathsf D_i(u)(\cdots)(m)=K_{B_{m2^m}}^{(i,u)}(\cdots)(m).
$$

Application below is left-associated:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf T&=\mathsf D_5(\mathsf D_4)(\mathsf D_3)(\mathsf D_2)(\mathsf D_1),\\
\mathsf W&=\mathsf D_6(\mathsf D_5)(\mathsf D_4)(\mathsf D_3)(\mathsf D_2)(\mathsf D_1),\\
\mathsf U&=\mathsf T(\mathsf T(\mathsf S)),\qquad
\mathcal Q=\mathsf U(\mathsf U(f_0)),\\
M&=\mathcal Q(8),\qquad Y=\mathsf W(\mathsf S)(f_0)(8).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

These integers do not depend on the spectrum index of $\eta$. The whole-type comparisons in the original tower paper give

$$
\mathsf W(\mathsf S)\ge\mathsf U^4,\qquad
\mathsf U\ge\mathsf S,\qquad
\mathcal Q\ge_3 H_{C[7]}.
\tag{9}
$$

Here $\ge_3$ quantifies over all final inputs at least 3 and all applicable lower-type arguments. It follows from exact safe-class BMS covers, tail locality, and explicit seed descent paths, not an inference from ordinal size. Whole-object fourfold slack and its propagation are proved in the [low-stack numerical comparison](../proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/hardy-root-low-stack-numeric.txt).

Since $\mathcal Q\ge f_0$, we have $M\ge f_0(8)=2048$. Strong prefix growth gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y&\ge\mathsf U^4(f_0)(8)
\ge\mathsf U(\mathcal Q)(8)
\ge\mathsf S(\mathcal Q)(8)\\
&\ge\mathcal Q(\mathcal Q(8))
=\mathcal Q(M)\ge H_{C[7]}(M).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

Section 3 turns the common entrance's 8-line into an actual $Y$-line with endpoint at most $\eta$. Critical counting from [Qi, Lemma 2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06733v5#S2) gives

$$
\boxed{\gamma_{2^Y}\le\eta=\gamma_s<\Sigma,\qquad s\ge2^Y.}
\tag{11}
$$

Thus the bound holds at $\eta$ itself, not merely at the looser $\Sigma$.

## 5. FGH with the specified fundamental sequences

Fix

$$
F_\varnothing(n)=n+1,\quad
F_{P+1}(n)=F_P^{\circ(n+1)}(n),\quad
F_P(n)=F_{P[n]}(n)\quad(P\text{ non-successor}).
$$

Hardy has empty value $n$, increments the argument at successors, and takes the original fundamental-sequence child at the same input otherwise. Write $A_{\rm I}=I[0][1]$ and $h=H_{A_{\rm I}}$. The exact original first-root substitution identity gives $H_{C[7]}(y)=G_{C[6]}^h(y)$, where $G$ uses $y$ successor iterations.

The [cross-input comparison, Sections 2–4](../proofs/scratch/iblp-I-to-I0-20261016/port-FGH-existing-bound-C6.txt) proves for this closed grammar that

$$
x\ge2,\quad y\ge x+1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
G_P^h(y)\ge F_P(x)+1.
\tag{12}
$$

The induction simultaneously quantifies over all $x,y$. Successors pair the two iteration trajectories, preserving a numerical difference of at least one. Limits use the exact literal prefix $P[x]\preceq P[y]$ and same-input prefix domination. Prefix preservation includes the frozen scan and marks. Arbitrary input or ordinal-index monotonicity of FGH or Hardy is not assumed. Termination rests on the original safe class and first-root closed grammar, with the full dependencies in [Section 3 of the repository's dependency index](../PROOF_DEPENDENCIES.zh-CN.md).

Apply (12) with $(x,y)=(M-1,M)$, $(2047,M)$, and $(6,M)$. Equations (10), (11) yield

$$
\boxed{s>F_{C[6]}(M-1)+1,\qquad
s>F_{C[6]}(2047),\qquad
s>F_{C[6]}(6)=F_C(6).}
$$

The final equality uses only the fixed limit clause $F_C(6)=F_{C[6]}(6)$.

## 6. Ranks, dependencies, and scope

The exact descent-tree ranks are

$$
\alpha_1=\Lambda=\lim(\mathrm{BMS}),\qquad
\alpha_{n+1}=\Lambda\omega^{\alpha_n},\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(C[n])=\alpha_n.
$$

If one additionally uses $\omega^\Lambda=\Lambda$, $\alpha_6$ simplifies to a right-associated tower of five $\Lambda$s. Numerical bounds always use the original fundamental sequences above; the rank name does not supply the numerical comparison.

The dependency chain is: the common entrance and new geometry (5)–(7); the archived front insertion, spare bands and natural relative BMS compiler; and the archived whole-type comparison and (12). Each reused manuscript and relevant section is linked above. Further cover and forest dependencies are in [Sections 2–3 of the repository's dependency index](../PROOF_DEPENDENCIES.zh-CN.md).

This does not prove a uniform contract for arbitrary type height, a bound for all inputs of the whole $C$, a full FGH bound for original $I[0][3][0]$, or a transfer from $\eta$ to the smaller $\xi$. Finite scripts do not turn the supremum, whole-object induction, or numerical comparison arguments into Lean theorems.
